# OSINT – Open Source Intelligence

**Definition**  
The disciplined collection, evaluation and analysis of information that is publicly available, legally accessible, and obtained without the need for covert or privileged access.

---

## Primary Data Sources

- Official government publications and statements
- Corporate and organisational disclosures
- Media reporting (with source evaluation)
- Academic and technical literature
- Public databases, registries and archives
- Social media and online forums (treated carefully)
- Imagery, maps and geospatial data that are openly published
- Technical artefacts that appear in public reports or passive observation

---

## Typical Collection Methods

- Systematic searching and monitoring of public sources
- Source triangulation and cross-verification
- Archival research (historical versions, leaks that are already public)
- Structured recording of provenance and collection date
- Passive technical observation (e.g. publicly visible infrastructure characteristics)

---

## Strengths

- Legally and ethically low-risk when properly scoped
- Often the only viable starting point for independent researchers
- Can establish high-confidence baselines (especially when official statements exist)
- Scales relatively well with good process
- Excellent for context, timelines and public narrative analysis

---

## Limitations and Failure Modes

- Incomplete or deliberately shaped public record
- High volume of noise and low-quality information
- Vulnerability to coordinated influence or false claims
- Difficulty establishing ground truth without corroboration
- Rapid decay of some online sources
- Legal and platform restrictions that change over time

---

## Common Biases

- Availability bias (over-weighting what is easy to find)
- Confirmation bias in source selection
- Over-confidence in official statements without checking consistency
- Treating volume of reporting as equivalent to quality of reporting

---

## Relationship to Other Disciplines

| Discipline | Typical interaction |
|------------|---------------------|
| TECHINT | OSINT often surfaces the initial indicators; TECHINT deepens the technical understanding |
| GEOINT | Location and imagery data frequently enter through open sources |
| SOCMINT | Social platforms are a major OSINT collection surface, but require separate analytical discipline |
| FININT | Corporate filings, sanctions lists and public financial data are classic OSINT sources |

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Define the collection question precisely before searching
- [ ] Record source, URL, access date and any observed changes
- [ ] Separate observed fact from interpretation
- [ ] Apply explicit confidence levels
- [ ] Seek independent corroboration for high-impact claims
- [ ] Document what could not be found as well as what was found
- [ ] Review legal and platform terms relevant to the sources used

---

## Confidence Notes

OSINT confidence is highest when multiple independent, high-quality public sources converge.  
It declines rapidly when relying on single unverified claims, anonymous posts, or sources with clear incentives to shape the narrative.
