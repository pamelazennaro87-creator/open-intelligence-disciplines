# Methodology & Standards

This document defines the working standards used across the repository.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Evidence-first**  
   Every factual claim must be traceable to a public source or explicitly marked as analytical judgment.

2. **Confidence levels**  
   - **High** – Multiple independent public sources or official statements  
   - **Medium** – Limited sources or logical inference from strong indicators  
   - **Low** – Single source, unverified claims, or speculative assessment  

3. **Operational honesty**  
   Methods are evaluated by whether they remain useful under real constraints (time, access, legal boundaries, incomplete data).

4. **No classification leakage**  
   Only publicly available information is used. No classified, non-public, or proprietary material is included or inferred beyond what is already open.

5. **Comparability**  
   Disciplines are described using consistent categories so they can be meaningfully compared.

---

## Standard Structure for Discipline Profiles

Each discipline file follows this template:

- Definition (precise, non-marketing)
- Primary data sources
- Typical collection methods
- Strengths
- Limitations and failure modes
- Common biases
- Relationship to other disciplines
- Practical checklist
- Confidence notes

---

## Standard Structure for Archive Cases

Each case study in `/archive` includes:

- Summary of the case
- Disciplines involved
- Key public sources
- What worked
- What failed or remained limited
- Lessons for multi-discipline analysis

---

## Visual Standards

Diagrams and matrices should:
- Clarify relationships or trade-offs
- Avoid decorative complexity
- Be reproducible from the accompanying text

---

## Language & Tone

- Professional and direct
- Avoid hype language (“powerful”, “cutting-edge”, “revolutionary”)
- Prefer precise terms over jargon
- English only
